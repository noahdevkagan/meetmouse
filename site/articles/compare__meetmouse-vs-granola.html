What each tool actually does

Granola sits quietly on your Mac (no bot joins the call), captures the audio, and blends your rough typed notes with an AI-written summary after the meeting. The output is a clean document: decisions, action items, context. It's a memory tool.

MeetMouse also sits quietly on your Mac with no bot — but it works in the opposite direction. While you're talking, it applies a coaching rubric to the live conversation and surfaces short cues in a small overlay: you're monologuing, you skipped the ask, that "sounds good" wasn't actually a commitment. The overlay never shows up in screen shares. It's a behavior tool.

One tells you what happened. The other changes what happens.

Where Granola wins

  • Notes. This is the whole category and Granola is excellent at it. MeetMouse doesn't compete here — it's not a notetaker.
  • Cross-platform. Windows, iOS, shared team folders. MeetMouse is Mac-only, on purpose.
  • Free tier for light users. Under ~25 meetings of history, Granola costs you nothing.

Where MeetMouse wins

  • It works during the meeting. A summary of the meeting where you talked 80% of the time doesn't fix the next one. A nudge at minute two does.
  • Privacy is absolute. Granola's notes run through cloud AI models. MeetMouse's transcription and AI run entirely on your Mac — turn WiFi off mid-meeting and it keeps coaching. For legal, healthcare, M&A, or anyone under NDA, that's not a feature, it's the requirement.
  • Pricing. $20 once vs $14/user/month forever. Granola Business costs more every seven weeks than MeetMouse costs ever.

My honest verdict

If you finish meetings and think "what did we decide?" — buy Granola, or just use their free plan.

If you finish meetings and think "why did I talk that whole time?" or "I never actually asked for the sale" — that's the problem I built MeetMouse for. And since neither app puts a bot in your call, they run side by side without conflict: Granola for the record, MeetMouse for the rep.
