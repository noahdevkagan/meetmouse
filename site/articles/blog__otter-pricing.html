The free plan's three limits

30 minutes per conversation. This is the one that bites. A one-hour client call gets cut in half. If your meetings run long, the free plan is really a trial.

300 minutes a month. About ten 30-minute calls. Fine for light use, gone in a week for anyone who lives in meetings.

Your 25 most recent conversations. Otter's whole strength is searching your history, and the free plan only keeps the last 25. You'd pay for Pro to get the thing Otter is best at.

Annual billing roughly halves Pro. If you know you'll use it for a year, don't pay monthly.

Students and teachers get 20% off Pro with an email address ending in .edu: $79.99 a year, or $13.59 a month. You sign up for the free plan with that address, then pick the student and teacher discount when you upgrade.

Is Otter worth paying for?

If you need to search months of transcripts, yes. Researchers, journalists and anyone who gets asked "what did they say in March?" get real value from Pro, and $8.33 a month on annual billing is cheap for that.

If you mostly want a summary after each call, you're paying for an archive you won't use. Fathom's free plan covers that.

When I'd pick something else

Otter joins as a bot and keeps your meetings in its cloud. If either of those is the problem, that's why I built MeetMouse: it listens on your Mac so nothing joins the call, transcription and AI run locally, and it nudges you during the call instead of just filing it away. Free for 3 meetings a week; $20 once for unlimited. It doesn't have Otter's team archive.
