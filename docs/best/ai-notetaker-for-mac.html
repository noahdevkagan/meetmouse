The best AI notetaker for Mac (from a guy who builds one)

By Noah Kagan · Founder of MeetMouse · September 29, 2026

TL;DR: Want bot-free notes your team can see? Granola. Want free and don't care about a bot? Fathom. Transcribing recordings offline? MacWhisper. Want nothing to leave your Mac and to actually get better in the meeting? That's mine, MeetMouse — $20 once.

Disclosure: I make MeetMouse, and it only runs on a Mac. So I'm biased, but I've also spent a stupid amount of time inside the exact macOS plumbing every one of these apps uses. Here's what actually matters.

There are two kinds of Mac notetaker. Native apps listen to your Mac's audio directly — no bot joins, and they hear anything: Zoom, Meet, Teams, FaceTime, a Slack huddle, a browser tab. Bot services send a participant into the call — works on any computer, but everyone sees it, and it can't hear FaceTime. Then there's one more question nobody asks until it matters: does the AI run on your Mac, or on their servers?

1. Granola — the best bot-free notes on a Mac

Granola made "no bot" a category. You type rough notes, it fills them in after the call, and they're genuinely good. The catch: notes are made with cloud AI, you need an account, and free history caps at 25 notes before it's $14/user/month. If you want the record and the cloud doesn't bother you, it's the default. My full comparison.

2. Fathom — free, if you'll put up with a bot

Best free plan in the category, period. A bot named Fathom joins your call and everything lives on their servers. It's the same on a Mac as anywhere else — which also means it can't hear your FaceTime calls.

3. Otter — the archive

Otter's been doing this forever and the searchable history is legit. But 300 free minutes a month is one busy Tuesday, and it's a bot plus the cloud.

4. MacWhisper — for files, fully offline

Point it at a recording and you get an accurate transcript without touching the internet. It's a transcription tool, not a meeting assistant — no live help, light on summaries. For journalists and researchers with a pile of audio, great value. MeetMouse vs MacWhisper.

5. Voice Memos + ChatGPT or Claude — $0

Record it, paste the transcript into an AI chat, type "summarize with action items." That's the tool. Clunky, free, and I did exactly this for months before building anything.

6. MeetMouse — the one I built

Every tool above tells you what happened after the meeting. I never read those summaries. My problem wasn't remembering meetings — it was talking over people and rambling past the close. So MeetMouse coaches you while it's happening: a short cue in a tiny overlay like "you've been talking for two minutes" or "that was a maybe, not a yes." The overlay doesn't show up in screen shares.

It writes notes now too — topics, next steps, who owns what — and you can ask any saved meeting a question. But everything runs on your Mac. Turn Wi-Fi off mid-call and it keeps going. No bot, no account, no telemetry.

Real talk on the limits: Mac only (macOS 14.2+). Apple Silicon recommended — on Intel it falls back to Apple's built-in transcription, which is less accurate. No team workspace. $20 once. If it doesn't change how your meetings go, email me within 30 days and I'll refund you.

Frequently asked questions

What's the best AI notetaker for Mac?

Granola for bot-free team notes. Fathom if you want free and don't mind a bot. MeetMouse if you want everything to stay on your Mac plus live coaching during the call, for $20 once.

Which Mac notetakers work offline?

MeetMouse (transcription and AI run on your Mac, so it works with Wi-Fi off) and MacWhisper (for recordings). Granola, Fathom, and Otter need the internet.

Why do Mac notetakers ask for Screen Recording permission?

That's how macOS gates system audio — the other side of the call. Bot-free apps like Granola and MeetMouse need it to hear the meeting. It doesn't mean they're recording your screen.

Does MeetMouse work on Intel Macs?

Yes, on macOS 14.2 or later, but Apple Silicon is recommended. On Intel, the high-accuracy transcription and speaker identification aren't available, so it uses Apple's built-in transcription with lower accuracy.
