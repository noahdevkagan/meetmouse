The best AI notetaker for Mac (from someone who builds one)

By Noah Kagan · Founder of MeetMouse · September 29, 2026

TL;DR: For bot-free notes that sync to a team, Granola. For notes that never leave your Mac plus live coaching during the call, MeetMouse — $20 once. For free and don't mind a bot, Fathom. For transcribing recordings offline, MacWhisper.

Disclosure: I make MeetMouse, a Mac-only meeting app. That makes me biased, and it also means I've spent a lot of time inside the exact APIs every Mac notetaker uses — ScreenCaptureKit for system audio, Core Audio for the mic, the Neural Engine for on-device models. So here's what actually separates them.

On a Mac, there are two kinds of notetaker. Native apps (Granola, MeetMouse, MacWhisper) listen to your Mac's audio directly — no bot joins the call, and it works with any app: Zoom, Meet, Teams, FaceTime, Slack huddles. Bot-based services (Fathom, Otter, Fireflies) send a participant into the meeting; they work the same on any OS but everyone sees them. The second question is where the AI runs: on your Mac, or on their servers.

1. MeetMouse — the only one that coaches you during the call

I'll get mine out of the way. MeetMouse listens through your Mac's audio (no bot), transcribes on-device, and runs a local AI model — it works with Wi-Fi off. While you talk, it shows short cues in an overlay that's hidden from screen shares: you're monologuing, you skipped the ask, a concern came up that you haven't answered. After the call you get topic-by-topic notes with owners, and you can chat with any saved meeting.

Where it's weaker: Mac only (macOS 14.2+, Apple Silicon recommended — Intel Macs fall back to Apple's built-in transcription), no team workspace, no CRM sync. It's built for one person getting better, not an org building an archive. $20 once, no account.

2. Granola — the best bot-free notes on the Mac

Granola pioneered the "no bot" notetaker and it's polished. You type rough notes, it fills them in with AI after the call, and shared folders make it good for teams. Notes are generated in the cloud, it needs an account, and the free plan caps history at 25 notes before $14/user/month. If you want the record and are fine with the cloud, it's the default choice.

3. Fathom — the best free option, if you'll accept a bot

Fathom's free plan is the most generous in the category. The trade is a visible participant in every meeting and everything stored on their servers. Works identically on Mac, Windows, and anywhere else.

4. Otter — for the searchable archive

Otter's strength is years of searchable transcripts and a live transcript during the call. Free tier stops at 300 minutes a month; Pro is $16.99/month. Bot-based and cloud.

5. MacWhisper — for recordings, fully offline

MacWhisper runs OpenAI's Whisper models locally and is excellent at turning an audio file into an accurate transcript. It's a transcription tool rather than a meeting assistant — no live cues, lighter on summaries — but for journalists and researchers with recordings, it's great value.

6. Voice Memos + an AI chat — the $0 route

Record with Voice Memos or QuickTime, paste the transcript into ChatGPT or Claude, ask for action items. Clunky and manual, but free and bot-free.

What to look for in a Mac notetaker

  • Apple Silicon support. On-device models need the Neural Engine to be fast. Intel Macs work with most cloud tools but struggle with local ones.
  • System-audio capture, not a bot. Needs Screen Recording permission on macOS — that's normal, not sketchy.
  • Where the transcript goes. "We don't train on your data" is not the same as "we never receive your data."
  • Screen-share behavior. If a tool shows anything on screen during the call, check whether it appears in your share.

So which one?

  • Bot-free notes with team sharing: Granola.
  • Free and you don't mind a bot: Fathom.
  • Transcribing recordings offline: MacWhisper.
  • Nothing leaves your Mac, and you want to get better at meetings — not just document them: MeetMouse, $20 once.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best AI notetaker for Mac?

For bot-free team notes, Granola. For fully on-device notes plus live coaching during the call, MeetMouse ($20 once). For a free option that uses a bot, Fathom.

Which Mac notetaker works offline?

MeetMouse (transcription and a local AI model run on your Mac, so it works with Wi-Fi off) and MacWhisper (for transcribing recordings). Granola, Otter, and Fathom need an internet connection.

Do Mac notetakers need Screen Recording permission?

Bot-free Mac apps like Granola and MeetMouse need it to capture system audio — the other side of the call. It's how macOS gates audio capture; it doesn't mean the app records your screen.

Does MeetMouse work on Intel Macs?

Yes, on macOS 14.2 or later, but Apple Silicon is recommended. On Intel, the high-accuracy transcription engine and speaker identification aren't available, so it uses Apple's built-in transcription with reduced accuracy.
