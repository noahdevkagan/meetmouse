The best AI notetaker for lawyers: start with where the call goes

By Noah Kagan · Founder of MeetMouse · September 29, 2026

TL;DR: For client calls, the safest AI notetaker is one where the conversation never reaches a third party. MeetMouse transcribes and summarizes on your Mac (local AI by default, works offline, no bot), $20 once. If your firm has vetted and contracted with a cloud vendor, Granola or Otter with a proper agreement can work. Avoid free bot-based tools on privileged calls.

Disclosure: I make MeetMouse. I'm also not a lawyer, and nothing here is legal or ethics advice — check your jurisdiction's rules and your firm's policies. What I can tell you is how these tools actually move data, because that's the part vendor marketing tends to blur.

For lawyers the question isn't which summary is prettiest. It's: who else hears this conversation? A cloud notetaker sends audio or transcript text to the vendor's servers, and often to an AI provider behind them. A bot-based notetaker also announces itself in the meeting and may email a recap to everyone on the invite — including opposing counsel if they're on it.

1. MeetMouse — nothing leaves the laptop

MeetMouse captures the call through your Mac's audio, so no bot joins and no recording notice appears. Transcription runs on-device and the default AI model runs locally — you can turn Wi-Fi off mid-call and it keeps working. After the call you get notes with next steps and a chat over the transcript that cites timestamps. There's no MeetMouse account and no MeetMouse server holding your meetings.

Two honest caveats. First, if you choose to add your own Claude or OpenAI key in Settings, meeting text goes to that provider — leave it on local for client work. Second, it's Mac-only and single-user: no matter management integration, no firm-wide archive. The live coaching (talk balance, unanswered questions) is a bonus for depositions-prep calls and client intakes.

2. MacWhisper — offline transcription of recordings

If you already record (with consent) and just need accurate text, MacWhisper transcribes files entirely on your Mac. No summaries or live help to speak of, but nothing leaves the machine.

3. Granola or Otter — only through a firm-level agreement

Both are capable, and both process your conversations in their cloud. If your firm's IT and ethics counsel have reviewed the vendor's terms, retention, and sub-processors, and you're on a business plan with the right agreement, they're reasonable. Don't use personal free accounts for client matters.

4. Fathom — great product, wrong default for privilege

Fathom is excellent free software, but a visible bot, cloud storage, and recap emails make it a poor default for privileged conversations.

Questions to ask any vendor

  • Does audio or transcript text ever leave my device? To whom?
  • Which AI sub-processors see the content, and do they retain it?
  • What's the default retention, and can I delete it permanently?
  • Does the tool email recaps or share links automatically to attendees?
  • Does it announce itself in the meeting, and can it be muted per call?

So which one?

  • Solo and small-firm lawyers who want AI notes without a third party: MeetMouse.
  • Transcribing recorded interviews offline: MacWhisper.
  • Firms with a vetted enterprise contract: Granola or Otter business plans.

Frequently asked questions

Is it safe for lawyers to use AI notetakers?

It depends on where the conversation goes. Cloud notetakers send audio or transcripts to the vendor and often to AI sub-processors, which raises confidentiality questions. On-device tools like MeetMouse keep the conversation on your Mac by default. Check your jurisdiction's ethics guidance and your firm's policies.

Which AI notetaker doesn't send client calls to the cloud?

MeetMouse runs transcription and its default AI model on your Mac and works with Wi-Fi off. MacWhisper transcribes recordings locally. Granola, Otter, Fathom, and Fireflies process conversations in their cloud.

Do I need client consent to use an AI notetaker?

Recording and transcription consent rules vary by jurisdiction, and many lawyers disclose AI note-taking regardless. On-device processing reduces who receives the data; it doesn't replace consent obligations.

Can MeetMouse be used for depositions?

MeetMouse is built for live meetings and calls, not as a certified court record. It can help you prepare and take personal notes; use a court reporter for the official transcript.
