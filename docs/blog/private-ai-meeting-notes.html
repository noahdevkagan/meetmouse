Quick answer

"Private" gets thrown around loosely. There are three separate questions: does a bot join your call, does your audio leave your machine, and who can access what's stored. Most tools fail two of three. Granola fixes the bot. Only MeetMouse fixes all three — it's the one tool here where nothing ever leaves your Mac.

If you handle anything sensitive — candidate interviews, medical or legal conversations, M&A, customer data under NDA, or just 1:1s where people say real things — "we use an AI notetaker" is doing a lot of unexamined work. Here's how to actually evaluate privacy in this category, and how each tool scores.

The three questions that matter

1. Does a bot join the call? A visible recorder changes what people say — and in many contexts (two-party consent states, candidate calls) it's a legal and courtesy question too. Bot-free tools capture system audio on your machine instead.

2. Does audio or transcript leave your machine? "No bot" does not mean "no cloud." Most bot-free tools still send meeting content to cloud AI for processing. Once it leaves, you're trusting someone else's retention policy, breach surface, and subpoena response.

3. Who can see what's stored? Team workspaces mean your calls are readable by admins and managers. Sales tools pipe them into CRMs. Ask: who, besides me, can pull up this conversation?

If you need cloud features anyway

Sometimes the archive or the CRM sync is the job, and the right answer is a cloud tool used deliberately: turn on the shortest retention window offered, restrict workspace access, tell participants they're recorded, and keep genuinely sensitive calls off the tool entirely. Fathom free is the best value in that world; Otter if you need search; Fireflies if the CRM machine earns its keep.

If private actually means private

The test is simple: turn off WiFi. Does the tool still work? Every notetaker above fails it. Questions

What is the most private AI meeting notes tool?
MeetMouse is the only mainstream option where meeting audio never leaves your machine — transcription and AI run entirely on your Mac, with no bot and no account. Granola is the most private cloud notetaker (no bot), but it still processes content with cloud AI.
Do AI notetakers send your meetings to the cloud?
Almost all of them, yes — including bot-free ones like Granola, which generate notes with cloud AI. The exception is MeetMouse, which processes everything on-device and works with WiFi off.
Are AI meeting bots legal in two-party consent states?
Recording laws vary and this isn't legal advice — but a visible bot plus verbal disclosure is the common practice for recorded calls. On-device tools that transcribe without storing audio recordings sit in a different posture; consult counsel for regulated conversations.
How do I check if a meeting tool is really private?
Turn off WiFi mid-meeting. If the tool keeps working, your audio is being processed locally. If it breaks, your meeting is going to someone's servers — go read their retention policy.
