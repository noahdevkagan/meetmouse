Fathom alternatives: what to use instead (from a guy who competes with them)

Disclosure: I make MeetMouse. I've said in my own Fathom comparison that Fathom is the best free notetaker on the market, and I'm not walking that back here. Free, generous, polished.

TL;DR

Nobody leaves Fathom over price — it's free and good. People leave over the bot (get Granola), the cloud (get MeetMouse — nothing leaves your Mac), or because notes never made anyone better at meetings (also MeetMouse: it coaches you live, $20 once).

So why are you searching "Fathom alternative"? In my experience it's one of three things. The bot: a participant named Fathom joins every call, and some meetings — candidates, therapy-adjacent 1:1s, skittish enterprise buyers — change the moment it appears. The cloud: free means your meetings are the asset, and every recording lives on their servers. Or the ceiling: you have six months of beautiful summaries and the same bad meetings — notes describe the problem, they don't fix it. Here's the map, by reason.

1. Granola — same no-subscription spirit, no bot

The closest like-for-like swap if the bot is your issue. Granola captures system audio on your Mac — no visible participant, guests never know. Notes are still generated with cloud AI (this is not the privacy option), and the free plan caps history at 25 notes before $14/user/month. For bot-free notes it's the best there is.

2. Otter — when the archive is the point

Fathom's summaries are better-looking; Otter's cross-meeting search is stronger, and it's the tool researchers and journalists default to. Free tier is thin at 300 minutes/month; Pro $16.99/month. Still a bot, still cloud.

3. tl;dv — moments over minutes

If what you actually share with your team is "watch these 40 seconds," tl;dv's clipping beats everyone's. Free unlimited recordings that self-delete after 3 months; Pro $29/month.

4. Fireflies — when you need the CRM machine

Outgrowing Fathom into a sales org? Fireflies pushes calls into the CRM, scores them, and gives managers the dashboard. From $18/user/month. Individuals: skip. Comparison here.

5. The scrappy $0 option

QuickTime or a voice memo, into ChatGPT or Claude, "summarize with action items." No bot, no account, no cloud subscription — just you and a file. Clunky, functional, free.

6. MeetMouse — for the two problems notes can't fix

Problem one, privacy: MeetMouse is the only tool here where audio goes nowhere. Transcription and AI run entirely on your Mac — no bot, no account, works with WiFi off. If your calls involve anything you wouldn't paste into a stranger's cloud, this is the difference that matters. Problem two, the ceiling: a perfect summary of you burying your ask is still a buried ask. MeetMouse coaches live — quiet cues only you see, while there's still time to fix it. $20 once. It keeps no cloud archive on purpose; plenty of people run it alongside Fathom and let each do its job.

So which one?

  • Bot-free notes: Granola.
  • Archive and search: Otter.
  • Clip-sharing teams: tl;dv.
  • Sales org: Fireflies.
  • True privacy, or actually getting better at meetings: MeetMouse, $20 once.

Questions

Is any Fathom alternative as good for free?
Honestly, for pure free notetaking — no. Granola's free plan caps history at 25 notes, Otter's stops at 300 minutes a month, and tl;dv deletes free recordings after 3 months. Fathom's free plan is the most generous; people switch over the bot, the cloud, or wanting more than notes.
What's the best Fathom alternative without a bot?
Granola — it captures system audio on your Mac so no participant joins the call. MeetMouse is also bot-free and additionally keeps everything on-device.
Is there a Fathom alternative that doesn't store meetings in the cloud?
MeetMouse is the only option where nothing leaves your machine — transcription and AI run 100% on your Mac, and it works with WiFi off. Every notetaker on the list, including bot-free Granola, uses cloud AI.
Can a meeting tool make you better at meetings instead of just taking notes?
That's MeetMouse's whole premise: live cues during the call — talk balance, buried asks, vague commitments — instead of a summary after it. Notes document the meeting; coaching changes it.
